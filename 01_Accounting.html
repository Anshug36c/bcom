Unit 1: Accounting foundations & recording

Core notes / Samajh

Accounting is the information system that identifies, measures, records and communicates financial events. It helps owners, managers, lenders, government and investors make decisions. Distinguish bookkeeping (systematic recording) from accounting (recording plus classification, summarising, analysis and communication). Core assumptions: business entity (business separate from owner), money measurement, going concern, accounting period, historical cost, dual aspect and accrual. Conventions include consistency, prudence, materiality and full disclosure.

Key concepts & application

Accounting equation: Assets = Capital + Liabilities. Every transaction changes at least two accounts while keeping this equation balanced. Account types: personal (debit receiver, credit giver), real (debit what comes in, credit what goes out), nominal (debit expenses/losses, credit incomes/gains). Example: owner starts business with ₹1,00,000 cash → Cash A/c Dr 1,00,000; To Capital A/c 1,00,000. Source documents (invoice, receipt, voucher) support entries. Subsidiary books: cash book, purchases book, sales book, purchase/sales returns books, bills receivable/payable books and journal proper.

Exam-ready tip: Use a transaction table: identify accounts → classify → apply debit-credit rule → write journal entry with narration. Remember “DEAD CLIC”: Debit Expenses, Assets, Drawings; Credit Liabilities, Income, Capital. It is a memory aid; always check account type and dual aspect.

Detailed explanation / In depth

Accounting is a common language for communicating performance, position and stewardship. Internal users such as managers need timely detail for pricing, budgets and cost control; external users such as lenders and investors need comparable, credible statements. The accounting entity assumption keeps owner transactions separate from business transactions: drawings reduce owner’s equity, while business expenses reduce profit. The going-concern assumption supports recording long-lived assets for use over time rather than assuming immediate liquidation. The accrual basis records income when earned and expenses when incurred, not merely when cash moves. A journal gives a chronological audit trail; subsidiary books divide repetitive transactions by type. Purchases book records credit purchases of goods for resale, not cash purchases or assets; sales book records credit sales of goods, not cash sales. Returns outward/inward record returns to suppliers/from customers. Cash book is both a book of original entry and a ledger account for cash/bank; a contra entry affects both cash and bank. Journal proper captures opening/closing, transfer, rectification and uncommon transactions.

Worked application / Answer mein kaise likhein

A practical transaction-analysis routine is: (1) name every account affected, (2) classify each account, (3) decide whether it increases or decreases, (4) apply the debit/credit rule, (5) check the equation. Example: paid rent ₹12,000 in cash: Rent expense increases (debit), Cash decreases (credit). Received ₹5,000 commission: Cash increases (debit), Commission income increases (credit). Goods purchased for resale are Purchases, not an asset called “Furniture”; goods bought for use are an asset. This distinction is a common exam trap. The source voucher controls the quality of the entry: invoice for credit purchase, cash memo for cash sale, receipt for money received, debit/credit note for returns or adjustment. A narration should explain the business reason, not repeat the ledger names. Example: “Being goods purchased on credit from Riya Traders as per invoice…” If a question asks books of accounts, distinguish books of prime entry from ledger accounts and state what transaction each book captures.

Unit 2: Journal, ledger and trial balance

Core notes / Samajh

The journal is the book of original entry: transactions are recorded chronologically with date, particulars, ledger folio and debit/credit amounts. A ledger groups entries account-wise; posting transfers journal debits/credits into the relevant accounts. Balance an account by totaling both sides and carrying the difference down. A trial balance lists ledger balances on a date to test arithmetic agreement (total debits = total credits).

Key concepts & application

Illustration: bought goods for cash ₹8,000: Purchases Dr 8,000; To Cash 8,000. In ledger, debit Purchases and credit Cash. Trial balance agreement does not prove complete accuracy: errors of omission, principle, original entry, commission, compensating errors or reversal may remain undetected. Errors that disturb agreement can be temporarily placed in suspense account pending correction.

Exam-ready tip: In exam answers show journal columns neatly, carry correct folio references, and explain the limitation of trial balance. Practice at least one full cycle from source transaction to journal, ledger and trial balance.

Detailed explanation / In depth

After journalising, posting places each debit/credit into the relevant ledger account. Balancing finds the net difference; assets/expenses commonly show debit balances, while liabilities/capital/income commonly show credit balances. A trial balance is a list of balances at a date, not an account and not a financial statement. It can detect a one-sided posting or unequal amount, but cannot detect a complete omission, wrong account of the same side, error of principle, or equal compensating errors.

Worked application / Answer mein kaise likhein

Rectification depends on when the error is found. Before trial balance, correct the affected entries directly; after agreement, a suspense account may temporarily bridge the difference; after final accounts, prior-period rules may apply. Never “force” the trial balance by changing a real ledger balance without evidence. In a long problem, keep a posting checklist: journal total, ledger posting, balancing, trial balance columns and suspense balance.

Unit 3: Final accounts and receipts-payments

Core notes / Samajh

Trading Account finds gross profit: Net Sales − Cost of Goods Sold. Cost of goods sold = Opening inventory + Net purchases + direct expenses − Closing inventory. Profit & Loss Account starts from gross profit, adds indirect incomes and deducts indirect expenses to find net profit. Balance Sheet reports assets, liabilities and capital on a particular date. Receipts & Payments Account is a cash summary used by non-profit organisations; it records cash/bank receipts and payments, capital and revenue, regardless of period accrual.

Key concepts & application

Adjustments: closing stock (Trading credit and Balance Sheet asset); outstanding expense (add to expense and show liability); prepaid expense (deduct from expense and show asset); accrued income (add to income and show asset); income received in advance (deduct income and show liability); bad debts/provision; depreciation. Balance sheet equation: Assets = Capital + Liabilities. Receipts & Payments differs from Income & Expenditure: cash basis vs accrual basis; all cash items vs revenue items only; opening/closing cash vs surplus/deficit.

Exam-ready tip: Always state treatment twice for adjustments where appropriate. Make a working note for net purchases, net sales, COGS and adjusted expenses. Clearly label “for the year ended” for statements and “as at” for balance sheet.

Detailed explanation / In depth

The Trading Account isolates gross operating result from buying and selling goods. Net purchases = purchases − returns outward; net sales = sales − returns inward. Direct wages, carriage inward and import duty may form part of cost of goods available for sale; office salary and advertising are indirect expenses in Profit & Loss. Net profit increases capital; net loss reduces it. The Balance Sheet is a position statement on a date, with assets arranged by liquidity or use and liabilities by due status.

Worked application / Answer mein kaise likhein

Adjustment double effect example: closing inventory ₹20,000 appears on the credit side of Trading Account and as current asset in Balance Sheet (unless already included in trial balance). Outstanding wages ₹3,000 are added to wages and shown as liability. Prepaid insurance ₹1,200 is deducted from insurance expense and shown as asset. Depreciation is charged to P&L and reduces asset value (or appears in accumulated depreciation). State each treatment explicitly; do not bury adjustments in totals.

Unit 4: Depreciation and stock valuation

Core notes / Samajh

Depreciation allocates the depreciable amount of a tangible fixed asset over its useful life. Causes include wear and tear, passage of time, obsolescence and depletion. It is not a cash fund; it is an expense and reduction of carrying amount. Straight Line Method (SLM): annual depreciation = (cost − residual value) ÷ useful life. Written Down Value (WDV): depreciation = opening book value × rate. SLM gives equal annual charge; WDV gives declining charge.

Key concepts & application

Example: machine cost ₹1,00,000, scrap ₹10,000, life 5 years → SLM ₹18,000 per year. Under WDV at 20%, year 1 = ₹20,000; year 2 = 20% × ₹80,000 = ₹16,000. Inventory is generally measured at lower of cost and net realisable value. Cost formulas include FIFO (oldest costs issued first) and weighted average (average unit cost = total cost / total units).

Exam-ready tip: Compare methods by annual profit, asset book value and tax implications; do not say depreciation directly creates cash. For inventory numerical questions lay out units, rates and closing stock valuation in layers.

Detailed explanation / In depth

Depreciation is a systematic allocation, not a valuation exercise and not a cash reserve. Under SLM the depreciable amount is spread evenly, useful when service potential is consumed evenly. Under WDV a constant percentage is applied to diminishing book value, often reflecting higher early-period use or obsolescence. If an asset is bought or sold mid-year, apply the question’s time convention and clearly show months. Disposal requires removing original cost and accumulated depreciation and comparing proceeds with carrying amount to find profit/loss.

Worked application / Answer mein kaise likhein

Inventory valuation affects both profit and current assets: higher closing inventory generally lowers COGS and raises gross profit, all else equal. FIFO sends earliest purchase costs to issues and leaves recent costs in closing inventory; weighted average smooths price fluctuations. Under rising prices, FIFO typically produces lower COGS and higher closing inventory than weighted average, but actual results depend on layers. Use the lower-of-cost-and-net-realisable-value rule as a prudence check, not as permission to select whichever number improves profit.

Unit 5: Computerised accounting and Tally

Core notes / Samajh

Computerised accounting uses software to capture, classify, process and report financial data. Benefits: speed, accuracy checks, real-time reports, integration, backups and reduced repetitive work. Risks: unauthorised access, data loss, incorrect setup, malware and over-reliance; controls include role-based access, strong authentication, audit trail, backups, validation and reconciliation. Tally workflow: create company → configure financial year/features → create groups and ledgers → enter vouchers → verify reports.

Key concepts & application

Common vouchers: Payment, Receipt, Contra, Journal, Sales, Purchase, Credit Note and Debit Note. Groups classify ledgers for financial statements. Trial Balance, Day Book, Profit & Loss, Balance Sheet, Cash/Bank books and stock summaries support review. Computerised output is only as reliable as source documents, account mapping and controls.

Exam-ready tip: Practical checklist: verify company name and year; select correct voucher; choose ledger and tax/inventory details; check debit-credit totals; save; review Day Book and reports; back up data. Never share credentials or post transactions without source evidence.

Detailed explanation / In depth

A computerised package automates posting, balances and reports but does not replace accounting judgment. Correct chart-of-accounts setup, opening balances, inventory units, tax configuration and financial-year dates are essential. Input validation prevents some errors; access controls and audit trails help detect others. Reconcile cash book to bank statement, physical inventory to system records, and customer/supplier balances to statements. Keep backup copies in a separate secure location and test restore procedures.

Worked application / Answer mein kaise likhein

In Tally-style workflow, create the company and financial year, enable only relevant features, create groups/ledgers, enter opening balances, record transactions using the correct voucher, then inspect Day Book, Trial Balance, P&L and Balance Sheet. Practical viva prompt: explain why a purchase of a computer is normally entered as an asset purchase rather than Purchases A/c, and how a payment voucher differs from a purchase voucher. Always retain source vouchers and do not share login credentials.
