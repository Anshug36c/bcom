Unit 1: Foundations, markets and equilibrium

Core notes / Samajh

Business economics applies economic reasoning to business decisions. It is applied, micro-oriented, forward-looking and concerned with optimisation under constraints. Microeconomics studies consumers/firms/markets; macroeconomics studies aggregate output, inflation, employment and growth. A market brings buyers and sellers together; price coordinates decisions. Equilibrium occurs where quantity demanded equals quantity supplied. Risk is variability of outcomes; return is reward; profit is revenue less economic cost (including opportunity cost).

Key concepts & application

Demand is willingness and ability to buy at different prices during a period. Supply is willingness and ability to sell. A demand increase shifts the curve right; a price change causes movement along a curve. Equilibrium price clears the market. A binding price ceiling can create shortage; a binding floor can create surplus.

Exam-ready tip: Label axes and curves carefully; explain whether a change is movement or shift and identify the determinant causing it.

Unit 2: Demand, elasticity and forecasting

Core notes / Samajh

Law of demand: other things equal, lower price generally raises quantity demanded. Exceptions/qualifications include unusual inferior goods and prestige effects. Demand determinants: income, tastes, related-good prices, expectations, population and promotion. Price elasticity Ed = percentage change in quantity demanded ÷ percentage change in price (often reported absolute). Elastic >1, inelastic <1, unitary =1. Income elasticity indicates normal/inferior goods; cross elasticity positive for substitutes and negative for complements.

Key concepts & application

Elasticity depends on substitutes, necessity, budget share and time. Total revenue test: when demand is elastic, price cut tends to raise revenue; when inelastic, price cut tends to lower revenue. Forecasting methods: consumer surveys, sales-force opinion, expert/Delphi, trend projection, moving averages, regression and market experiments. Select method based on horizon, data, cost and product maturity.

Exam-ready tip: Use percentage change consistently; midpoint method avoids base-period asymmetry. A good forecast gives assumptions and a range, not false certainty.

Unit 3: Production and cost

Core notes / Samajh

Production transforms inputs into output. Factors: land, labour, capital and entrepreneurship. Production function links inputs to maximum output for given technology. Short run has at least one fixed factor; long run all factors variable. Law of variable proportions: as variable input rises with fixed input, marginal product may first rise, then diminish, and can eventually fall. Isoquant shows input combinations yielding same output; isocost shows same expenditure. Least-cost combination occurs at tangency (MRTS = input-price ratio).

Key concepts & application

Costs: fixed, variable, total, average and marginal. In short run, AFC falls as output rises; MC intersects AVC and ATC at their minimum points. Long-run average cost may decline with economies of scale, flatten, then rise with diseconomies. Explicit costs are paid; implicit costs are opportunity costs of owned resources. Private cost is borne by decision-maker; social cost includes external effects.

Exam-ready tip: Keep total/average/marginal concepts distinct. Explain why marginal cost intersects average cost at its minimum: below average it pulls average down; above average it pushes average up.

Unit 4: Market structures and pricing

Core notes / Samajh

Perfect competition: many firms, homogeneous product, free entry/exit; firm is price-taker. Monopoly: one dominant seller, entry barriers, price-maker with demand constraints. Monopolistic competition: many firms, differentiated products, non-price competition. Oligopoly: few interdependent firms; strategic reactions matter. Price discrimination charges different prices for the same product when markets can be separated and resale restricted; it requires market power and different demand elasticities.

Key concepts & application

Under perfect competition, long-run economic profit tends toward normal profit with entry/exit. Monopoly may restrict output and charge above marginal cost; outcomes depend on demand and costs. Oligopoly strategies can include price leadership, collusion (where illegal), and non-price rivalry. Avoid asserting a single universal oligopoly outcome.

Exam-ready tip: Use a comparison table by number of sellers, product, entry barriers, price control and examples. Explain conditions before describing price discrimination.

Unit 5: Growth, cycles, BOP and inflation

Core notes / Samajh

Economic growth is sustained rise in real output; development also includes structural and human-welfare changes. Business cycle phases: expansion, peak, contraction/recession and trough/recovery. Balance of Payments records transactions between residents and rest of world: current account (goods, services, income, transfers) and capital/financial flows. Inflation is sustained rise in general price level, reducing purchasing power. Demand-pull inflation arises when aggregate demand exceeds capacity; cost-push from rising input costs.

Key concepts & application

Inflation impacts households, firms, savers, borrowers and external competitiveness differently. Measures include CPI and WPI (each has scope/weighting differences). Policy responses may include monetary tightening, fiscal measures and supply-side steps; trade-offs include output/employment effects. Business-cycle indicators help firms plan inventory, investment and cash.

Exam-ready tip: Separate growth from development, and current account from financial flows. In policy answers state the cause first—demand-pull vs cost-push—then match remedy and mention side effects.
