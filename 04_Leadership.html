Unit 1: Leadership concepts and development

Core notes / Samajh

Leadership is a process of influencing people toward shared goals; it is not identical to formal authority. Myths: leaders are born only, one style fits all, charisma guarantees results, or leadership is a solo act. Interactional view examines leader, followers and situation together. New leaders’ first 90 days: learn context, build relationships, set priorities, secure early wins and adapt. Action–Observation–Reflection (AOR): act, observe consequences, reflect on assumptions, then improve the next action.

Key concepts & application

Leader development grows an individual’s capability; leadership development strengthens collective capacity and relationships. Effective transition requires listening, stakeholder mapping, realistic priorities and feedback. Early wins should build credibility without neglecting long-term systems.

Exam-ready tip: Use specific behaviours and context, not personality labels alone. Reflective practice turns experience into learning.

Unit 2: Attributes, personality and emotional intelligence

Core notes / Samajh

Leadership attributes include integrity, competence, confidence, persistence, curiosity and humility. Trait approaches identify tendencies, but no trait guarantees success in every setting. Personality types can help discuss preferences, not diagnose or box people. Intelligence supports analysis; emotional intelligence (EI) includes self-awareness, self-regulation, motivation, empathy and social skill. EI helps leaders read emotion, manage reactions and build trust.

Key concepts & application

Example: during a deadline conflict, self-aware leader notices frustration, pauses, asks each member about constraints, clarifies the shared goal and negotiates a fair plan. Cultural and individual differences mean the same behaviour may be interpreted differently.

Exam-ready tip: Compare trait and behavioural approaches. Explain EI with a practical workplace example and avoid claiming emotional skill replaces technical competence.

Unit 3: Power, influence, ethics and behaviour

Core notes / Samajh

Power sources include legitimate, reward, coercive, expert and referent power. Influence may use rational persuasion, consultation, inspirational appeal, collaboration or coalition; ethical influence is transparent and respects agency. “Doing the right things” includes balancing results with principles. Character-based leadership relies on integrity, fairness, courage and accountability. Values guide decisions when rules do not specify an answer.

Key concepts & application

Leadership behaviours may be task-oriented (clarifying work, standards) or relationship-oriented (support, listening, development); effective balance depends on circumstances. Leadership pipeline involves transitions in skills and time focus as responsibility grows. Multi-rater/360 feedback combines perspectives from manager, peers, reports and self; confidentiality, behaviour-based prompts and coaching make it useful.

Exam-ready tip: Power is capacity; influence is the process of using it. Distinguish ethical persuasion from manipulation and state safeguards for 360 feedback.

Unit 4: Delegation, empowerment and team leadership

Core notes / Samajh

Delegation assigns responsibility and appropriate authority while preserving manager accountability. Empowerment adds information, competence, discretion and confidence so people can act. Team effectiveness needs a clear purpose, complementary skills, norms, trust, psychological safety, accountability and feedback. Leader actions: clarify roles, remove obstacles, model respectful disagreement, recognise contribution and resolve conflict early. Offsite team development can build trust but must connect to real work and follow-up.

Key concepts & application

Virtual teams need explicit communication norms, documented decisions, inclusive meeting practices, time-zone fairness and intentional relationship-building. Multicultural teams benefit from curiosity, clear language, checking assumptions and multiple channels for input.

Exam-ready tip: Do not confuse empowerment with leaving people unsupported. Match authority to responsibility and define escalation boundaries.

Unit 5: Team processes, coaching and conflict

Core notes / Samajh

Team processes include communication, coordination, decision-making, trust and shared learning. Coaching asks focused questions, listens, agrees next steps and follows up rather than simply giving answers. Team decisions can use consensus, majority, leader decision with input or expert judgment; choose based on urgency, risk and expertise. Conflict may be task, process or relationship-based. Address interests and facts; avoid personal attacks.

Key concepts & application

Conflict process: clarify issue → hear perspectives → identify shared goals → generate options → agree owner/deadline → review. Virtual and multicultural teams face risks of silence, misread tone and unequal participation. Build inclusive turn-taking, written summaries and psychological safety. Great teams learn from setbacks and adapt without losing accountability.

Exam-ready tip: For a case answer identify conflict type, root cause, intervention, owner and follow-up measure. Healthy task disagreement can improve decisions when respect is maintained.
