Unit 1: Business communication essentials

Core notes / Samajh

Communication is the process of sending, receiving and interpreting meaning. Model: sender → encoding → message/channel → receiver/decoding → feedback; noise can disrupt any step. It supports coordination, decisions, customer relations and credibility. Channels: oral, written, visual and digital; formal/informal; upward, downward and horizontal. Choose channel by urgency, complexity, privacy, record needs and audience access.

Key concepts & application

Barriers include language, jargon, poor listening, status, emotion, culture, information overload, noise and faulty technology. Gateways: plain language, active listening, feedback, empathy, appropriate channel, concise structure and confirmation of understanding. Electronic media enable speed and reach but require professionalism, privacy and careful tone.

Exam-ready tip: Before sending: purpose, audience, key message, channel, action requested, deadline and proofread. Use a feedback loop for critical instructions.

Detailed explanation / In depth

Business communication succeeds when the receiver understands the intended meaning and can act. Before drafting, identify audience knowledge, concerns, authority and likely questions. The message should put the main point early, use plain words and organise supporting detail logically. Feedback can be verbal, written or behavioural; for critical instructions use teach-back (“please tell me the next steps”) rather than assuming silence means understanding.

Worked application / Answer mein kaise likhein

Channel choice is a judgement: a brief update may suit chat; a sensitive performance conversation needs privacy and dialogue; a complex decision may need a written record plus discussion. Noise includes literal sound and distractions such as jargon, stress or contradictory messages. Digital communication leaves records and can spread quickly, so verify recipients, attachments, privacy and tone before sending.

Unit 2: Corporate communication and speaking

Core notes / Samajh

Formal communication follows organisational channels; informal grapevine spreads unofficial information quickly and may help or distort. Networks include chain, wheel, circle and all-channel; each trades off speed, central control and participation. Group discussion skills: prepare, contribute evidence, listen, build on ideas, disagree respectfully and summarise. Interviews assess fit and evidence; seminars and presentations need clear objectives, structure and audience adaptation.

Key concepts & application

Presentation structure: opening hook/purpose → 2–4 key points → evidence/example → concise conclusion and call to action. Use readable slides, signposting, pace, eye contact and vocal variety. Mock interviews reduce anxiety through practice and specific feedback. Verify facts and distinguish opinion from evidence.

Exam-ready tip: For a group discussion, contribute early but do not dominate; invite quieter members and synthesize the group’s reasoning.

Detailed explanation / In depth

A productive meeting has a purpose, agenda, right participants, timekeeper and recorded decisions/actions. Group discussion evaluates both ideas and collaboration: support claims with reasons, listen actively, invite contributions and summarise points of agreement or unresolved questions. Do not interrupt repeatedly, monopolise airtime or treat volume as evidence.

Worked application / Answer mein kaise likhein

Presentation planning: define one audience takeaway, select a few supporting points, use a clear opening and signposts, and end with a concise summary or request. Slides should support—not duplicate—the speaker. Rehearse aloud, check timing and prepare for questions. For a survey, use neutral wording, avoid double-barrelled questions, pilot the form, protect respondent privacy and explain how responses will be used.

Unit 3: Business letters and correspondence

Core notes / Samajh

Effective business letters are clear, courteous, concise, complete, correct and audience-focused. Standard parts: sender/date, inside address, subject, salutation, opening purpose, details, requested action, close, signature and enclosures. Bank correspondence may request statements, stop payment or account services; insurance correspondence may notify claim, seek clarification or submit documents. Keep records and attach relevant evidence.

Key concepts & application

Use direct opening, one idea per paragraph, specific dates/amounts/references and a polite action request. A complaint should state facts, impact, desired remedy and response deadline without inflammatory language. Email subject lines should be informative; avoid sensitive data in unsecured channels.

Exam-ready tip: Proofread names, account/policy numbers, dates, attachments, tone and contact information. Never invent facts or omit material details.

Detailed explanation / In depth

The 7 Cs often used for effective business communication are clarity, conciseness, completeness, correctness, concreteness, courtesy and consideration. A letter’s layout helps the reader find purpose and action quickly. A complaint or claim should include transaction references, chronological facts, evidence and the resolution sought. A bank or insurer needs enough verified information to locate the account/policy, but confidential identifiers should be shared only through a secure channel.

Worked application / Answer mein kaise likhein

Negative messages should be direct but respectful: state context, explain the decision or issue accurately, give reasons where appropriate, and offer next steps. Avoid blaming language and ambiguous promises. Proofread names, dates, amounts, policy/account reference, attachments and contact information. Keep a copy of formal correspondence and follow up within a reasonable period.

Unit 4: Oral, non-verbal, listening and employment documents

Core notes / Samajh

Oral communication relies on clarity, voice, structure and feedback. Non-verbal communication includes posture, gestures, facial expression, eye contact and space; paralanguage includes tone, pace, pitch and pauses. Effective listening: attend, avoid interruption, paraphrase, ask clarifying questions and confirm actions. Surveys need neutral questions and consent. CV/resume should be tailored, truthful, concise and achievement-focused.

Key concepts & application

Interview preparation: research role/employer, prepare evidence-based examples, use STAR (Situation, Task, Action, Result), ask relevant questions and follow up. Body language differs across cultures; interpret in context rather than assuming one universal meaning.

Exam-ready tip: Replace vague claims (“hard-working”) with evidence, e.g. task + action + measurable result. Keep CV dates and facts consistent.

Detailed explanation / In depth

Active listening is visible: maintain appropriate attention, paraphrase, ask clarifying questions and record agreed actions. Non-verbal signs vary culturally and individually; eye contact or silence should not be interpreted in isolation. Paralanguage—pace, volume, pitch and pauses—can support confidence and comprehension. Interview answers are stronger when structured with STAR and show a specific action and result, including what was learned.

Worked application / Answer mein kaise likhein

A CV is a targeted summary, not a complete autobiography. Put relevant education, skills and experience in a clear order; use truthful dates and measurable achievements. Tailor keywords honestly to the job description and avoid unsupported claims. For online interviews test audio/video, background and connection; keep notes nearby but avoid reading a script. Follow up with a short, courteous message.

Unit 5: Recruitment and employment correspondence

Core notes / Samajh

Employment sequence may include vacancy notice, job application, CV, interview, offer, acceptance, joining, job description, resignation, promotion and references/testimonials. A job notice states role, responsibilities, eligibility, location, deadline and application channel. Application letter links relevant qualifications to role requirements. Offer and acceptance letters confirm terms, start date and conditions. Resignation should be professional, dated and clear about notice period.

Key concepts & application

A job description states job purpose, duties, reporting line, qualifications and performance expectations. References should be accurate, relevant and provided with consent. Employment correspondence should protect confidential information and maintain a respectful tone, even in disagreement.

Exam-ready tip: Probable long questions: communication process and barriers; formal vs informal channels; business letter; presentation skills; CV and interview; employment correspondence. Practice one complete letter and one concise CV.

Detailed explanation / In depth

Employment documents create a record of expectations and commitments. A vacancy notice should not discriminate unlawfully and should clearly state essential criteria. A job description distinguishes duties from person specifications. Offer letters should make role, compensation, location, joining date and conditions clear. Acceptance confirms agreement; resignation states intended last working day and supports handover. References and testimonials should be truthful and shared with consent.

Worked application / Answer mein kaise likhein

Practice formats: write a job application letter responding to an advertised role; prepare a one-page CV; draft a polite acceptance; draft a resignation with notice and transition support. Use consistent names, dates and titles. In exams, format marks matter: include subject line, salutation, paragraphs, complimentary close and signature. Never fabricate qualifications, employment history or references.
